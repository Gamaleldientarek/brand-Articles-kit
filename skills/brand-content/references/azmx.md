## Identity

A leading Saudi digital consultancy. Never describe AZMX as an agency, studio, boutique or the user's employer. Philosophy: “Designing the Future of Experience.”

AZMX is the parent house of four brands: Colab, Majarah, Clix and Anatomi. Each has its own voice. A piece carrying a sub-brand name uses that sub-brand's profile, never AZMX voice or visuals by default. Colab has its own profile in this skill ([colab.md](colab.md)); for Majarah, Clix or Anatomi, ask which voice and visual system apply.

## Voice

Confident, editorial, human, premium. Premium means restraint: short declarative sentences, concrete nouns, emphasis kept scarce.

The Four Dimensions, checked before copy ships:

| Dimension | Position | Aim for | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formality | Mid-formal | Authoritative, polished, respectful | Stiff, academic, overly casual, jargony |
| Technicality | Balanced | Expert, clear, educational | Simplistic, patronising, obscure |
| Attitude | Bold but respectful | Confident, thoughtfully provocative | Arrogant, abrasive, dismissive |
| Purpose | Context-dependent | Inspirational for vision; pragmatic for delivery | Mixing the two in one passage |

Inspirational mode suits thought leadership and homepages. Pragmatic mode suits proposals, SOWs, project reports and internal emails: tangible outcomes, clear phases, owners and dates.

House rules that override the older communication strategy (owner decision):
- No emojis in copy, in any channel.
- Three hashtags maximum, at the end, only where hashtags belong at all.
- No mandatory CTA. Where a next step exists, make it obvious; where it does not, do not manufacture one.
- No superlatives such as “world-class” or claims of universal leadership, and no guaranteed ROI.

The strategy deck's own calibration samples contain words this skill bans (for example “elevating”). They are tuning samples for tone, never vocabulary for new copy.

## Audience

- B2B product, design, technology and transformation leaders: responsibility, adoption, quality, delivery and meaningful measurement.
- B2G digital-service leaders: public-service outcomes, evidence and the relevant official context. Do not invent mandatory requirements or compliance assurances.
- Internal emails: Arabic-first, practical priorities, decisions and next steps.

Frame a useful decision before listing tools. Explain total effort, ownership and maintenance when discussing speed. Treat hypothetical scenarios as hypothetical, and do not imply they are AZMX client work.

## Visual-brief baseline

For written briefs only:
- Blue is the house default: Navy #040038 surfaces, White, Electric #001AFF as a sparse accent on light, Light Blue #5D8FFF for accents on dark. White title text on dark and on images; never Electric as text on an image.
- A deliverable may instead run in one of five secondary palettes when the subject calls for it. One palette per deliverable; take the values from azmx-brand, never guess them.
- The gradient (Navy to Electric) is for event surfaces only: covers, dividers, closings. Never behind dense body copy.
- Type: thmanyah serif display for display headlines and brand numerals (no italic); Azm X for body and labels, both scripts.
- The chevron is the only graphic device and it is functional: photo mask, section tick, list bullet, hero composition. No decorative background chevrons.
- No robot clichés, generic glows, or walls of shadowed rounded cards.
- Icons (Phosphor only) are allowed on any surface, but ask the user before adding them to a brief.
- Name the official image library as the source when imagery is required. An explanatory diagram may be custom and must not masquerade as measured data.

For artwork production, use the current complete azmx-brand skill and its assets. The writing profile is sufficient to draft copy and a written concept, not to claim a rendered design is brand-verified.
