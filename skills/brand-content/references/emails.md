# Emails and newsletters

Determine brand, sender identity, audience, purpose, language and whether this is a personal email, newsletter or sequence. Use supplied recipient data only. Do not import a sender address or recipient from examples or previous clients.

Deliver:
- subject: specific, accurate and useful; approximately 35–50 characters is a starting point, not a hard cap;
- preheader for newsletters/HTML campaigns, adding information rather than repeating the subject;
- body with the main point early;
- one clear next step when relevant;
- sign-off consistent with the verified sender.

For a short individual email, do not add a preheader, SEO plan, image brief or HTML unless needed. For a sequence, state each message's job and write each message fully. Timing is proposed unless the user authorizes scheduling.

No emojis in subject lines or body copy, no exclamation-mark urgency and no em-dashes. A brand's email design system may use visual chip glyphs as components; those are layout, not copy.

AZMX: connect the message to the decision, delivery or outcome. Colab: connect it to the research question, evidence or learning. Do not fabricate urgency, open rates, testimonials, audience segments or personalised facts.

Arabic and English editions should sound natural on their own. Preserve offers, dates, amounts, terms and qualifications exactly. Ask when a consequential commercial detail is missing; do not invent it to finish the CTA.

For HTML production only, load the brand's actual email design system/assets and the environment's document/email tooling. This writing skill does not itself guarantee email-client compatibility or deliverability.

Review subject/body consistency, links, attachments, names and requested action. Sending, campaign activation and scheduling are separate actions requiring current explicit authorization. Preparing a draft is not authorization to send it.
