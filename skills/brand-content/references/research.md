# Research method: from a topic to a defensible article

Read this for research-led articles or a requested claim audit. For an email edit or a supplied-copy rewrite, use only the relevant checks; do not launch a full research project by default.

## 1. Establish the decision and source material

Read the actual card, linked draft/report and existing language editions. State the reader's problem and the article's central question. Separate what the user supplied from what needs external verification.

Inventory report title, language, author, date and available attachments. A linked Arabic PDF is not an existing Arabic/English article. If extracting a PDF, inspect suspicious passages against the page before blaming the source for extraction errors. Do not claim to have reviewed visual layout from extracted text alone.

If supplied material is unreadable, identify the missing source and complete independent work; do not invent its contents or a review verdict.

## 2. Turn the topic into answerable questions

Use questions that will change the article, for example:
- What can the tool or method actually do today?
- What evidence supports the claimed benefit, and in which conditions?
- What changes for a team, user or decision-maker?
- What remains uncertain or requires human evaluation?
- What Saudi or Arabic context materially affects the answer?

For AZMX, connect the answers to responsibility, adoption, delivery and measurement. For Colab, connect them to observable behaviour, research methods and the limits of evidence. Use the same source where appropriate without turning both brands into the same article.

## 3. Search in deliberate passes

Start with primary sources: current product documentation, original research papers, standards bodies and relevant official Saudi sources. Search in English for technical specifications and studies; search Arabic when local terminology, service context or search intent matters.

A practical query progression:
- topic + official documentation;
- claimed metric or report name + original study;
- topic + limitations / evaluation / methodology;
- relevant authority + the local requirement or concept;
- Arabic and English reader questions for search-intent context.

Search queries are discovery, not evidence. Open the actual supporting page and read the relevant passage. Check publication/update dates against the event or version being discussed. A newly updated page can describe an old study.

Use professional secondary analysis to explain a debate or discover primary evidence; identify its nature. Vendor examples show what the vendor reports, not an independent universal benchmark. Do not manufacture competing viewpoints when the evidence is consistent.

## 4. Audit claims before outlining around them

For important factual claims, retain concise working notes:
- claim or question;
- exact supporting source URL and source type;
- source date/version where meaningful;
- what it supports;
- limitation or required editorial action.

Verify percentages, denominators, samples, scope and comparisons. Ask what was measured and whether the result transfers to the article's context. Check “caused” versus “associated with”, time saved versus total cost saved, and component reuse versus user outcomes.

A broad design-performance study is not a causal estimate of Design System ROI. A hypothetical labour-cost calculation is not realised savings. Include implementation, migration, support and maintenance where an actual financial discussion requires them.

A general bibliography homepage does not substantiate a specific percentage. Trace the original source; otherwise remove or qualify the claim. Never preserve a questionable figure merely because it appears prominently in the user's report.

## 5. Read the keyword inventory alongside the research

Use the supplied workbook's brand, language and intent fields, not just matching words. Record the sheet/row or stable keyword ID and data source date. Preserve blanks as unknown and historical values as historical.

Select a primary intent plus supporting phrases and related topics. Label any new English or Arabic phrases as proposed seeds unless independently validated. A lack of a topic cluster in the workbook is not evidence that nobody searches for it.

Review representative search results to understand the question and prevalent content forms. Describe this as exploratory when you do not have location-controlled ranking data. Do not claim a complete competitor audit, search volume or absence of competition from a few results.

See [SEO](seo.md) for placement, metadata, links and measurement. Keyword analysis can refine the angle; it must not force irrelevant sections.

## 6. Build the argument and stop research at a useful point

Research is sufficient to draft when:
- the central question can be answered responsibly;
- the claims the article will rely on have inspected supporting sources;
- important counterexamples/limitations are understood;
- unresolved claims are excluded, qualified or isolated as publication dependencies;
- a distinct angle and practical example can be explained without fabricating evidence.

There is no magic minimum number of citations. Continue if a material assertion is unresolved; stop collecting repetitive sources when they no longer change the argument. Record what remains uncertain and do not label it verified.

## 7. Produce the research brief and editorial plan

The research brief should contain:
1. Topic, reader and central question.
2. Recommended angle and why it suits this brand.
3. The main supported findings with direct sources and limitations.
4. Corrections needed in supplied material.
5. Keyword cluster and provenance.
6. Article versus Blog Post decision.
7. Outline, illustrative example and readiness/dependencies.

Keep it separate from the article body. Use the [research-plan template](../assets/research-plan.md) as a guide, not a requirement to fill every box for a narrow request.

## 8. Carry evidence into both language editions

Write original explanations and connective reasoning; do not closely imitate one source's structure or prose. Label hypothetical scenarios and proposed measurement frameworks. Never invent participant data, quotations, client stories or the author's first-person experiences.

Place citations near the supported claim using direct links. Respect the environment's quotation and paraphrase limits across Arabic and English together. Preserve qualifications in adaptation: “may” must not become “will”, or a vendor example become an independent study.

Check fast-changing tool/standard claims again near actual publication if the draft has aged. An article corrected today does not mean its source PDF was repaired. If a download asset has unsupported claims, document the remaining dependency and identify its language correctly.
