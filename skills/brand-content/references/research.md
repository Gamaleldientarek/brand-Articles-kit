# Research and evidence

Research to resolve the topic's actual questions. Browse current primary/reputable sources when requested or when facts may have changed. Prefer official product documentation, original studies, standards bodies and relevant Saudi authorities. Review dated vendor cases as vendor evidence, not independent benchmarks.

Keep compact working notes: claim, direct URL, what the source supports, important limitation. Do not turn this into a rigid ledger that overwhelms the article.

Check percentages, sample sizes, dates, causal wording, comparisons and financial assumptions. Trace a PDF's claim to the underlying source. A homepage bibliography does not validate a specific percentage. If a source cannot be checked, remove the unsupported claim or explicitly qualify it outside the final factual assertion.

Label hypothetical examples and suggested measurement frameworks. Do not invent lived experiences, data, author approvals or client results. Respect source quotation and paraphrase limits across both language editions.

Citations belong near supported factual claims. Avoid decorative citations at the end of unrelated sections. General advice can be identified as the author's recommendation without pretending it came from a study.

If public download depends on a flawed source report, distinguish: article revised; source report unchanged; download/publication pending correction. Do not silently edit the source PDF, publish it or imply the English article provides an English PDF.
