# Research and editorial-plan template

Use for a research-led article. Replace these instructions with real findings; keep this working plan outside the reader-facing article.

## Brief and decision

Identify brand, source material, author, audience, central question, intended takeaway and requested languages. Recommend Article or Blog Post and explain why. State the target length for each edition.

## Research questions and findings

List the questions that materially affect the article. For each major finding include a direct inspected source, what it supports and its limitation. Keep unresolved report claims and required corrections separate. Record source dates/versions when they matter.

## Keyword group

Identify the inventory file/sheet/row or keyword ID, language, intent and source date. Select a primary intent and supporting phrases. Distinguish existing historical data, fresh validated data and new candidates without metrics.

## Angle and outline

State the angle and its difference from related content. Give each section a job, evidence/example and approximate word allocation when useful. Plan a connected example without inventing client results. Identify the source asset and language for any download CTA.

## Delivery plan

Name the requested editions, metadata and document tabs. Assign the cover and internal image different jobs. Record the actual card fields/status mapping only in private task notes, not in this distributable template.

## Ready to write

Explain whether the core claims are supported, what remains unresolved, and whether it blocks drafting, design or publication. Do not turn unavailable facts into placeholders inside final copy.
